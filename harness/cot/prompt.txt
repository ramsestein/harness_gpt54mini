You are an expert anesthesiologist in the middle of a real surgical case. Below is the current state of the patient, derived from continuous intraoperative monitoring.

{case}

You must choose the single best next anesthetic adjustment among these five options, with the drug each one acts on:

a) increase_hypnotic  - increase the hypnotic (propofol) infusion rate
b) reduce_hypnotic    - reduce the hypnotic (propofol) infusion rate
c) increase_opioid    - increase the opioid (remifentanil) infusion rate
d) reduce_opioid      - reduce the opioid (remifentanil) infusion rate
e) no_action          - make no infusion-rate change at this moment

Do not answer straight away. Work the case the way you would at the head of the table: alternate between what you still need to establish (Thought) and what the case actually shows (Action, then Observation). Reason on three axes and let each one drive the next query:

1. haemodynamics - MAP, HR, their trend, and how the hypnotic and the opioid bear on them;
2. depth of hypnosis - BIS, the propofol effect-site concentration, and the recent hypnotic changes;
3. analgesia - the remifentanil effect-site concentration, the recent opioid changes, and the nociceptive signs.

Available actions. You have no tools and the case above is all the information that exists; each Action is a self-query that you answer from that text.

- LOOKUP <variable>              current value of a monitored variable, e.g. LOOKUP map_current
- TREND <signal>                 recent direction of a signal, e.g. TREND bis
- HISTORY <drug>                 recent infusion-rate changes of a drug, e.g. HISTORY propofol
- COMPARE <option> vs <option>   weigh two of the five options against each other

Response format. Repeat this block once per thing you need to establish, at most four times:

Thought: <what you still need to establish, and why it changes the decision>
Action: <exactly one of the four actions above>
Observation: <the value, trend or history that the case above actually shows>

Then close with exactly these two lines, and write nothing after them:

Thought: I now have enough information to decide.
Answer: <letter>

Rules.

- Every Observation must be read from the case above. Never invent, round or carry over a number from another patient.
- The trend matters as much as the current value: a MAP of 60 that is rising is not the same problem as a MAP of 60 that is falling, and the same holds for BIS and for the drug effect-site concentrations.
- Do not decide on a single variable. A change of hypnotic is only right if the depth of hypnosis is the problem, and a change of opioid is only right if the analgesia is the problem.
- Consider no_action seriously: if the patient is stable on the three axes, the best adjustment is often to wait.
- If two options look equally good, use COMPARE on them before deciding.
